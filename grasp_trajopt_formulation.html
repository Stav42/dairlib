Main section · code & practical operation

Current implementation, from executable to experiment.

The remainder of this document is source-checked and operational: the state layout, C3 equations as the code assembles them, executor torque laws, reference handling, contact changes, gait behavior, and the commands and diagnostics used to run the system. It describes what the executable does now—not a proposed design or a theoretical alternative.

01 · Snapshot

What runs now

The program reaches a synthesized multi-finger grasp, detects first contact from simulation, settles through a short pinned-cube handoff, and then controls a free cube with a receding-horizon complementarity controller. It can hold a static grasp, follow a moving cube reference, deliberately alter the contact set, or perform a slow finger-relay gait.

Source of truth

The executable entry point is examples/sampling_c3/allegro_grasp_c3_squeeze.cc, built as //examples/sampling_c3:allegro_grasp_c3_squeeze. The 19-line runtime parses and validates flags, snapshots them into an immutable SqueezeConfig, and calls SqueezeApplication::Run(). Ordinary .h/.cc components then own simulation (SimulationEnvironment), grasp geometry and IK (GraspSetup), the continuous AutoDiff linearization plant (LcsModel), C3 construction and solves (C3Planner), and release/gait transitions (ManeuverController). There are no textual .inc implementation fragments, no giant reference tuple, and every Allegro squeeze implementation file is below 500 lines.

Simulation plant

Drake discrete SAP contact simulation at 1 ms, using the right Allegro hand and a free 6 cm cube.

Planning plant

A separate continuous Drake plant with the same generalized-coordinate layout produces the local C3 model.

Default path

task_space execution: task-space fingertip PD, normal grip force, and the C3 first control move after release.

Optional branches

Moving cube references, live contact IK, middle/ring release and regrasp, and a deliberately slow gait sequence.

02 · Plant & state

One state layout feeds simulation, linearization, and control.

The right Allegro hand provides 16 actuated joints. The cube is free floating, represented by a quaternion and translation in configuration plus a six-dimensional spatial velocity. The controller uses generalized actuation torques.

\[ q = \begin{bmatrix}q_{\mathrm{hand}} \in \mathbb{R}^{16}\\ q_{\mathrm{cube}} \in \mathbb{R}^{7}\end{bmatrix}, \qquad v = \begin{bmatrix}v_{\mathrm{hand}} \in \mathbb{R}^{16}\\ v_{\mathrm{cube}} \in \mathbb{R}^{6}\end{bmatrix}, \qquad x = \begin{bmatrix}q\\v\end{bmatrix} \in \mathbb{R}^{45}, \qquad \tau \in \mathbb{R}^{16}. \]
ComponentImplemented role
SimulationDiscrete Drake SAP at a 1 ms step. Contact results are also used for the handoff latch.
C3 modelContinuous plant, locally linearized around the measured state and current contact configuration.
Cube frameAll programmed translations and rotations are defined from the grasped cube state; the target cube is rendered as a ghost.
Contact geometryDefault contact intent is index/middle on −Y and thumb on +Y. Optional topology enables ring participation.

Grasp IK uses fingertip surface offsets before solving: 11.5 mm along local tip z for index, middle, and thumb; the ring additionally uses a 5 mm local y offset. Each target point is constrained independently within ±1 mm.

03 · Reach & handoff

The program earns contact before asking C3 to control it.

The initial joint posture comes from grasp IK. Fingers begin about 1 cm outside their intended cube surfaces and follow a cubic joint-space approach, normally lasting 5 s. Default commanded penetration is 5 mm at the thumb and zero at index and middle.

\[ q(t) = q_0 + \bigl(q_{\mathrm{contact}} - q_0\bigr) \left(3s^2 - 2s^3\right), \qquad s = \operatorname{clamp}\!\left(\frac{t}{T_{\mathrm{approach}}},0,1\right). \]
  1. After the approach has started, ContactResults latches once the contact threshold is reached (default 0.02).
  2. The controller waits for a short dwell (default 0.15 s), then constructs C3 around the seated state.
  3. For 0.5 s after that handoff, the cube remains pinned while the controller settles.
  4. The cube is released and the selected executor begins controlling the free-cube system.
Meaning of the pin

The temporary pin is an implementation bridge between a detected simulation contact and the local controller setup. It is not a physical grasp model or a claim that contact has converged perfectly.

04 · C3 replanning

The planner solves a local complementarity-constrained finite-horizon problem.

At each planning update, the continuous plant is linearized and discretized around the measured state and the active fingertip–cube contact pairs. C3 then solves the resulting local Linear Complementarity System (LCS) over a short horizon using ADMM. The solve returns a planned state trajectory, scaled torque inputs, contact variables, and complementarity gaps; the executor consumes their first knot.

\[ x_{k+1} = A x_k + B_s \tilde{u}_k + D\lambda_k + d, \qquad \eta_k = E x_k + F\lambda_k + H\tilde{u}_k + c, \qquad 0 \leq \lambda_k \perp \eta_k \geq 0. \]

Variables and local model

SymbolMeaning in this implementation
\(x_k\)The full 45-state prediction at knot \(k\): 16 hand positions, 7 cube configuration coordinates, 16 hand velocities, and 6 cube velocities.
\(\hat{x}\)The measured simulation state supplied as the equality-constrained first knot: \(x_0 = \hat{x}\).
\(\tilde{u}_k\)C3's internally scaled 16-vector of actuator inputs. It is converted to physical generalized torque before direct playback.
\(\lambda_k\)The nonnegative contact-variable vector. Its size \(n_\lambda\) is created from the selected contact model, number of active contacts, and friction-direction count.
\(\eta_k\)The complementarity-gap vector paired elementwise with \(\lambda_k\).
\(A,B_s,D,d\)Locally linearized, discrete dynamics matrices and offset. \(D\) maps the contact variables into the next state.
\(E,F,H,c\)The local affine contact map that produces \(\eta_k\) from state, contact variables, and input.
\(k\)A C3 knot. With the defaults, controls/contact variables run from \(0\) through \(N-1\), while states run from \(0\) through \(N\), where \(N=5\).

The complementarity relation is elementwise. For every paired entry \(j\), it means that both quantities are nonnegative and their product vanishes:

\[ \lambda_{k,j} \geq 0,\qquad \eta_{k,j} \geq 0,\qquad \lambda_{k,j}\eta_{k,j} = 0. \]

Thus a contact-force channel can be positive only when its paired gap/slack is zero; if the channel has positive gap/slack, the paired force is zero. This is the non-convex contact-mode choice that distinguishes the C3 problem from an ordinary tracking QP.

Contact variables and Stewart–Trinkle friction

The default contact model is stewart_and_trinkle, with friction coefficient \(\mu=0.5\) and two friction directions per contact. In the implementation's Stewart–Trinkle indexing, the stacked variable is ordered as:

\[ \lambda_k = \begin{bmatrix} \gamma_k \\ \lambda_{n,k} \\ \beta_k \end{bmatrix} \in \mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{n_\lambda}. \]

Here \(\lambda_{n,k}\) contains the normal contact-force magnitudes, \(\beta_k\) contains the nonnegative friction-pyramid coefficients, and \(\gamma_k\) is the associated nonnegative sliding/slack block. The exact length of \(\beta_k\) changes with the active contact count and num_friction_directions. The LCS factory encodes the normal, friction-pyramid, and sliding relations into the single \(\eta_k\) map above. In particular, the tangential force is represented by a nonnegative combination of fixed tangent directions:

\[ f_{t,k}^{\,i} = D_t^{\,i}\beta_k^{\,i}, \qquad \mathbf{1}^{\mathsf T}\beta_k^{\,i} \leq \mu\,\lambda_{n,k}^{\,i}, \qquad \lambda_{n,k}^{\,i} \geq 0, \]

for each active contact \(i\). The default three-finger configuration uses index, middle, and thumb. A topology change rebuilds the LCS with its new active set, so \(n_\lambda\), the contact pairs, and the normal-force index groups are regenerated rather than masked downstream.

Finite-horizon problem actually assembled

At a high level, C3 minimizes state tracking and physical torque effort, subject to the LCS, the measured initial state, complementarity, and torque boxes. The optional force-reference term is disabled by default.

\[ \begin{aligned} \underset{\substack{x_{0:N},\,\tilde{u}_{0:N-1}\\ \lambda_{0:N-1},\,\eta_{0:N-1}}}{\operatorname{minimize}} \quad & \sum_{k=0}^{N} (x_k-x_k^{\mathrm{des}})^{\mathsf T}Q_k (x_k-x_k^{\mathrm{des}}) \\ &+ \sum_{k=0}^{N-1} \tilde{u}_k^{\mathsf T}R_k\tilde{u}_k + \sum_{k=0}^{N-1} (\lambda_k-\lambda_k^{\mathrm{des}})^{\mathsf T} W_{\lambda,k}(\lambda_k-\lambda_k^{\mathrm{des}}) \\ \text{subject to}\quad & x_0=\hat{x}, \\ &x_{k+1}=Ax_k+B_s\tilde{u}_k+D\lambda_k+d, \\ &\eta_k=Ex_k+F\lambda_k+H\tilde{u}_k+c, \\ &0\leq\lambda_k\perp\eta_k\geq0, \\ &-\frac{\tau_{\max}}{s_u}\leq\tilde{u}_k\leq \frac{\tau_{\max}}{s_u}. \end{aligned} \]

The last line is a per-joint box constraint. It is the only explicit input constraint placed in the current C3 problem; joint-position and joint-velocity bounds are not added as optimizer constraints.

What the state and force costs mean

The diagonal state cost is constructed by block. Cube pose (quaternion plus translation) receives w_cube; hand joint positions for active fingers receive k_hold; hand velocity receives w_vel; and cube velocity receives w_cube_vel. At the defaults, only cube pose is weighted strongly: w_cube = 10000, while k_hold, w_vel, and w_cube_vel are zero.

When w_lambda > 0, C3Plus adds the last quadratic term in the objective. Its physical normal-force targets are:

\[ \lambda_{n,\mathrm{index}}^{\mathrm{des}} = \alpha_m,\qquad \lambda_{n,\mathrm{middle}}^{\mathrm{des}} = \alpha_m,\qquad \lambda_{n,\mathrm{thumb}}^{\mathrm{des}} = 2\alpha_m. \]

The code maps those targets through the active-contact normal groups and converts them to C3's internal contact scale before calling SetForceTrackingWeight and UpdateForceTarget. With the default w_lambda = 0, that term is absent: the planner instead has to preserve the cube-pose target against the gravity contained in the local dynamics, and the required contact forces emerge through the LCS constraints.

Input and contact scaling

C3 optimizes a scaled input, not physical torque directly. On the first construction, the program chooses \(s_u\) from --input_scale when it is positive, otherwise from the ratio of the local matrix norms. It then multiplies the LCS input matrix by that scale and chooses \(R_k\) so the optimizer still penalizes physical torque effort:

\[ \tau_k = s_u\tilde{u}_k,\qquad B_s = s_u B,\qquad R_k = s_u^2 w_R I. \]

The input scale remains fixed through later relinearizations and topology rebuilds. Contact variables have a separate internal scaling \(s_\lambda\), maintained by C3 when it scales complementarity dynamics. Force-reference construction accounts for that scale so its requested values retain their physical meaning.

ADMM split and receding-horizon timing

The affine trajectory constraints and quadratic costs form the QP side of the ADMM iteration. The non-convex piece is a projection of the paired contact variables onto the complementarity set. The augmented-Lagrangian matrices controlled by w_G and w_U regulate this consensus process; they are solver penalties, not cube-tracking weights or physical grasp-force targets.

\[ \mathcal{C} = \left\{(\lambda,\eta)\ \middle|\ \lambda\geq0,\ \eta\geq0,\ \lambda\odot\eta=0 \right\}. \]

At the defaults, the local model has \(N=5\), \(\Delta t=0.05\,\mathrm{s}\), and therefore a \(0.25\,\mathrm{s}\) prediction window. Relinearization and C3 solve cadence default to 40 simulation steps (40 ms), while the SAP simulation advances at 1 ms. Between solve events, the previously computed first input/force plan remains available to the executor. ADMM uses 20 iterations by default with rho_scale = 1.2; optional within-solve and across-solve warm starts are separately configurable.

05 · Execution

Three executors expose different ways to consume the same setup.

ModeTorque actually applied after release
directOnly the first C3 command, converted with the input scale: \(\tau = s_u\tilde{u}_0\). No task-space PD, grip, or gravity term is added.
task_spaceThe default. Fingertip task-space PD toward the contact IK pose, a normal-direction grip term, and the scaled C3 move. Optional gravity compensation is separately enabled.
oscJoint PD toward a filtered desired posture, gravity torque, and a normal force proxy from the C3 force solution. This branch does not add C3 u₀.

All three paths write a 16-vector of generalized hand torque to the same actuator input. The symbols below are shared: \(q_h\) and \(v_h\) are measured hand position and velocity; \(p_i\) is fingertip \(i\)'s world position; \(J_{h,i}\) is the fingertip translational Jacobian restricted to the hand's 16 velocity columns; and \(R_{WC}\) is the measured cube orientation. Therefore,

\[ \dot{p}_i = J_{h,i}v_h, \qquad n_i = R_{WC}d_i, \qquad d_{\mathrm{index}}=d_{\mathrm{middle}}= \begin{bmatrix}0\\1\\0\end{bmatrix}, \quad d_{\mathrm{thumb}}= \begin{bmatrix}0\\-1\\0\end{bmatrix}. \]

The vectors \(d_i\) are fixed inward press directions in the cube frame; multiplying by \(R_{WC}\) makes the grip direction rotate with the cube. In the optional four-finger topology, the ring uses the same \(+Y\) cube-frame press direction as index and middle. Regardless of executor, the final C3-phase command is hard-clamped per joint to the physical torque bound:

\[ \tau_{\mathrm{applied},j} = \operatorname{clamp}\!\left( \tau_{\mathrm{cmd},j}, -\tau_{\max}, \tau_{\max} \right), \qquad j=1,\ldots,16. \]

direct — C3 input playback

This is the cleanest test of what the C3 optimizer itself commanded. C3 optimizes the scaled input \(\tilde{u}_0\), so the executor converts it once to physical generalized torque and applies nothing else:

\[ \tau_{\mathrm{direct}} = s_u\tilde{u}_0. \]

There is no PD posture term, Jacobian-transpose fingertip force, force floor, or explicit gravity compensation. Gravity is already represented in the LCS drift used to create \(\tilde{u}_0\), so adding a separate gravity term would change the experiment. Unlike the other hybrid path, this mode uses the same equation during the pinned warm-up and after cube release.

task_space — task-space PD, C3 grip, and C3 input feedforward

This is the default post-release executor. It first selects one desired point per fingertip. With the default fk_target=false, these are the fixed three grasp points on the nominal cube; with fk_target=true, they are the forward kinematics of C3's planned next hand configuration \(q_{h,1}^{\mathrm{C3}}\):

\[ p_i^{\mathrm{des}} = \begin{cases} p_{i,\mathrm{grasp}}(X_{WC0}), & \mathtt{fk\_target}=\mathrm{false},\\[3pt] \operatorname{FK}_i\!\left(q_{h,1}^{\mathrm{C3}}\right), & \mathtt{fk\_target}=\mathrm{true}. \end{cases} \]

The grip magnitude is taken from the first projected ADMM consensus vector, not from the ordinary C3 input solution. Physically, this is C3's contact-side answer to: “how hard should this fingertip press into the cube right now?” The projection has enforced the nonnegative/complementary contact choice, so the executor reads its normal-force entry and turns it into an inward fingertip push.

\[ \lambda^{\delta}_0 = \operatorname{GetDualDeltaSolution}(0) \big|_{\lambda}, \qquad \lambda_{n,i}^{\delta} = \sum_{j\in\mathcal{N}_i}\lambda_{0,j}^{\delta}, \] \[ f_{n,i}^{\mathrm{des}} = \max\!\left( \max\!\left(0,\lambda_{n,i}^{\delta}\right), f_{i,\mathrm{floor}} \right), \] \[ f_{\mathrm{index},\mathrm{floor}} = f_{\mathrm{middle},\mathrm{floor}} = \mathtt{force\_floor}, \qquad f_{\mathrm{thumb},\mathrm{floor}} = 2\,\mathtt{force\_floor}. \]

\(\mathcal{N}_i\) is not a physical vector or the dynamics matrix \(A\); it is simply the set of array slots in C3's stacked contact vector that belong to finger \(i\)'s normal force. The letter \(j\) above is only an array index. For the default Stewart–Trinkle model with index, middle, and thumb active, each group has one normal-force slot:

\[ \mathcal{N}_{\mathrm{index}}=\{3\},\qquad \mathcal{N}_{\mathrm{middle}}=\{4\},\qquad \mathcal{N}_{\mathrm{thumb}}=\{5\}, \] \[ \lambda_{n,\mathrm{index}}^{\delta}=\lambda_{0,3}^{\delta}, \qquad \lambda_{n,\mathrm{middle}}^{\delta}=\lambda_{0,4}^{\delta}, \qquad \lambda_{n,\mathrm{thumb}}^{\delta}=\lambda_{0,5}^{\delta}. \]

So, in the ordinary three-finger case, there is no meaningful “sum” to picture: the code reads one normal-force value per fingertip. The grouping notation merely keeps the same code correct for other contact models or a changed active set, where one physical normal force can be represented by more than one C3 variable. At the default force_floor=0, the nonnegative projected value is used directly; a positive floor says “keep pressing with at least this much normal force even if the current C3 projection asks for less.” The code then forms a Cartesian fingertip force consisting of positional PD and inward normal grip:

\[ f_{i,\mathrm{task}} = k_{p,\mathrm{task}}\left(p_i^{\mathrm{des}}-p_i\right) - k_{d,\mathrm{task}}\dot{p}_i + f_{n,i}^{\mathrm{des}}n_i, \] \[ \tau_{\mathrm{task\_space}} = \tau_{g,\mathrm{optional}} + \sum_{i\in\{\mathrm{index,middle,thumb}\}} J_{h,i}^{\mathsf T}f_{i,\mathrm{task}} + s_u\tilde{u}_0. \]

Here \(k_{p,\mathrm{task}}\) and \(k_{d,\mathrm{task}}\) are the task_kp and task_kd flags (defaults 70 and 0). The final term is the raw first C3 input used as feedforward. The gravity term is either \(\tau_g\), computed from the hand's gravity generalized forces, or zero according to exec_grav_comp; it is zero by default because the C3 input already comes from a gravity-containing LCS.

There is one deliberate exception before the cube unpins. During that warm-up period, task_space does not use the post-release law above. It holds the grasping joints at the contact-IK posture while C3 solves in the background:

\[ \tau_{\mathrm{pinned}} = k_{p,\mathrm{reach}} \left(q_{h,\mathrm{contact}}-q_h\right) - k_{d,\mathrm{reach}}v_h + \tau_g. \]

This separate preload prevents the pinned cube from making a near-zero C3 solution appear sufficient while the fingertips drift away.

osc — joint PD, gravity, and normal-force feedforward

Despite its name, the current osc branch is not operational-space control and does not call inverse dynamics. It chooses a joint target, filters a finite-difference target velocity, applies joint-space PD plus gravity, and adds a Jacobian-transpose force from C3's first planned contact variable.

The joint target is selected as follows. auto chooses live grasp IK when track_cube_contact is on, otherwise C3's next state knot; the ik and c3 settings force either choice. The live-IK target is ramped between discrete IK solves so it does not step abruptly into the PD law:

\[ q_h^{\mathrm{des}} = \begin{cases} q_{h,\mathrm{contact}}^{\mathrm{live}}, & \mathtt{osc\_target\_source}=\mathtt{ik},\\ q_{h,\mathrm{contact}}^{\mathrm{live}}, & \mathtt{osc\_target\_source}=\mathtt{auto} \ \land\ \mathtt{track\_cube\_contact},\\ q_{h,1}^{\mathrm{C3}}, & \text{otherwise}. \end{cases} \] \[ q_h^{\mathrm{des}}(t) = (1-r)q_{h,\mathrm{live}}^{\mathrm{previous}} + r q_{h,\mathrm{live}}^{\mathrm{current}}, \qquad r = \operatorname{clamp}\!\left( \frac{t-t_{\mathrm{IK}}}{T_{\mathrm{IK}}},0,1 \right) \]

During a release, regrasp, or gait leg, the moving finger's corresponding four-joint segment is overridden by its release, regrasp, or gait trajectory. A disengaged relay ring is held at its parked joint posture. These are direct changes to \(q_h^{\mathrm{des}}\), so the same PD law below drives both ordinary tracking and the finger-transfer motions.

\[ \dot{q}_{h,\mathrm{raw}}^{\mathrm{des}} = \frac{q_h^{\mathrm{des}}(t)-q_h^{\mathrm{des}}(t-\Delta t)} {\Delta t}, \qquad \dot{q}_{h,\mathrm{filt}}^{\mathrm{des}} \leftarrow \dot{q}_{h,\mathrm{filt}}^{\mathrm{des}} + \alpha\left( \dot{q}_{h,\mathrm{raw}}^{\mathrm{des}}- \dot{q}_{h,\mathrm{filt}}^{\mathrm{des}} \right), \qquad \alpha=\operatorname{clamp}\!\left( \frac{\Delta t}{\tau_{\mathrm{filter}}},0,1 \right), \] \[ \tau_{\mathrm{PD}} = k_{p,\mathrm{osc}}\left(q_h^{\mathrm{des}}-q_h\right) - k_{d,\mathrm{osc}} \left(v_h-\dot{q}_{h,\mathrm{filt}}^{\mathrm{des}}\right). \]

The default gains are osc_kp=300, osc_kd=15, and a 50 ms target-velocity filter. The \(k_d\) term damps error relative to the moving target rather than treating every desired motion as an error.

For the grip term, C3's first returned force vector is divided by its current contact scale, summed over each contact's normal-force group, optionally cross-faded when a finger has just joined, and then mapped into joint torque:

\[ \lambda_0^{\mathrm{osc}} = \frac{\operatorname{GetForceSolution}(0)}{s_\lambda}, \qquad \bar{f}_{n,i} = \sum_{a\in\mathcal{N}_i}\lambda_{0,a}^{\mathrm{osc}}, \] \[ f_{n,i}^{\mathrm{osc}} = \mathtt{lambda\_torque\_scale} \left[ (1-r_i)f_{n,i}^{\mathrm{crossfade}} + r_i\bar{f}_{n,i} \right], \qquad r_i=\operatorname{clamp}\!\left( \frac{t-t_{i,\mathrm{joined}}}{T_{\mathrm{force\_ramp}}},0,1 \right), \] \[ \tau_{\mathrm{force}} = \sum_{i\in\mathrm{active}} J_{h,i}^{\mathsf T}\left(f_{n,i}^{\mathrm{osc}}n_i\right), \qquad \tau_{\mathrm{osc}} = \tau_g+\tau_{\mathrm{PD}}+\tau_{\mathrm{force}}. \]

Crucially, \(\tilde{u}_0\) does not appear in \(\tau_{\mathrm{osc}}\). This executor uses C3's next hand configuration and planned normal-force entries, but it does not replay C3's planned torque input. Its force term contains only the configured normal direction; it does not reconstruct tangential friction force, a full six-dimensional wrench, or the exact nonlinear inverse-dynamics realization of the C3 plan.

Important distinction

Choose direct to isolate the C3 input trajectory itself. Choose task_space for the default assisted grasp, where C3 input, fingertip PD, and projected-contact grip all contribute. Choose osc when its joint-target and normal-force-proxy realization are specifically wanted; it is a distinct controller, not an inverse-dynamics QP.

06 · Moving references & live IK

The command, the real cube, and the finger target are deliberately separate.

When a cube motion is requested, the program maintains two poses: a pose it wants the cube to have and the pose the simulator says the cube actually has. A third pose is derived only for finger IK, so the fingers do not race far ahead of a cube that is lagging or slipping.

1. Programmed cube target: what the task asks for

The “programmed target” is simply a time-indexed desired cube pose, \(X_{WC}^{\mathrm{cmd}}(t)\). It is generated by cube_motion_mode: static hold, a min-jerk step, or a sine wave, with configurable x/y/z translation and roll/pitch/yaw offsets from the initial cube pose \(X_{WC0}\). In gait mode, the gait state machine supplies the rotation target instead.

\[ X_{WC}^{\mathrm{cmd}}(t) = \operatorname{cube\_target\_pose}(t). \]

This is a reference, not a command that teleports the physical cube. C3 is penalized for departing from it, but contact dynamics still determine what the actual cube does. At each C3 knot, the raw programmed pose and translational velocity populate the cube portion of \(x_k^{\mathrm{des}}\). Desired angular velocity is currently left at zero.

Ghost cube

The translucent cyan cube in Meshcat is drawn at \(X_{WC}^{\mathrm{cmd}}(t)\). It is a visual ruler: it shows where the task wants the cube, not a second physical object. It has no collision geometry, contact force, or influence on the simulation.

Measured cube

\(X_{WC}^{\mathrm{meas}}(t)\) is read from the simulated cube state. It is the opaque cube visible in Meshcat and can lag, drift, or rotate differently from the cyan ghost because the grasp must physically transmit the requested motion.

2. Clamped live reference: how far the fingers may lead

With track_cube_contact=true, the program does not point the fingers directly at the raw programmed target if that target is far from the actual cube. Instead it starts from the measured pose and moves only a bounded distance toward the command. This produces the IK-safe pose \(X_{WC}^{\mathrm{IK}}\).

\[ \Delta p = p^{\mathrm{cmd}}-p^{\mathrm{meas}}, \qquad p^{\mathrm{IK}} = p^{\mathrm{meas}} + \min\!\left(1,\frac{d_{\max}}{\lVert\Delta p\rVert}\right)\Delta p, \qquad d_{\max}=\mathtt{cube\_ik\_lead\_pos\_max}. \]

Rotation is treated the same way: the relative rotation from \(R^{\mathrm{meas}}\) toward \(R^{\mathrm{cmd}}\) is limited to cube_ik_lead_rot_max. The defaults allow at most 5 cm of translational lead and 0.3 rad (about 17.2°) of rotational lead.

\[ X_{WC}^{\mathrm{IK}} = \operatorname{ClampLead}\!\left( X_{WC}^{\mathrm{cmd}}, X_{WC}^{\mathrm{meas}}; d_{\max},\theta_{\max} \right), \qquad \theta_{\max}= \mathtt{cube\_ik\_lead\_rot\_max}. \]

For example, if the task asks the cube to move 20 cm but the real cube has barely moved, the cyan ghost appears 20 cm ahead while the fingers are allowed to aim only 5 cm ahead of the real cube. That prevents the hand controller from letting go of the cube to chase an unreachable future pose. This clamp affects the finger IK only: the ghost and C3's cube-pose cost still use the raw task command. The difference is therefore useful evidence that the reference is outrunning the physical grasp.

3. Live grasp IK: turn that safe pose into finger joints

Each contact footprint \(r_i^C\) is stored in cube coordinates. The program maps it through the clamped IK pose, then solves grasp IK for the hand posture that places every active fingertip at its corresponding point:

\[ p_{i}^{\mathrm{IK}} = X_{WC}^{\mathrm{IK}}r_i^C, \qquad q_{h,\mathrm{live}} = \operatorname{GraspIK}\!\left( \left\{p_i^{\mathrm{IK}}\right\}_{i\in\mathrm{active}} \right). \]

There are therefore two clamped IK poses at every live-IK update. “Start” means the C3 horizon's current knot, not the original pose at the beginning of the experiment. “End” means its final horizon knot, \(N\Delta t\) later. Both are clamped relative to the same cube pose measured now:

\[ X_{WC,0}^{\mathrm{IK}} = \operatorname{ClampLead}\!\left( X_{WC}^{\mathrm{cmd}}(t_{\mathrm{now}}), X_{WC}^{\mathrm{meas}}(t_{\mathrm{now}}) \right), \] \[ X_{WC,N}^{\mathrm{IK}} = \operatorname{ClampLead}\!\left( X_{WC}^{\mathrm{cmd}}(t_{\mathrm{now}}+N\Delta t), X_{WC}^{\mathrm{meas}}(t_{\mathrm{now}}) \right). \]

The program does construct a desired cube pose at every horizon knot before the solve:

\[ X_{WC,k}^{\mathrm{cmd}} = X_{WC}^{\mathrm{cmd}}(t_{\mathrm{now}}+k\Delta t), \qquad k=0,\ldots,N. \]

Those are the cube entries written into C3's desired-state trajectory. They are not the same thing as C3's predicted cube states \(X_{WC,k}^{\mathrm{C3}}\), which are only produced after C3 solves the optimization. Using those predicted states to build the hand reference would make the reference depend on the solution it is supposed to help determine, and a local C3 prediction is not automatically a geometrically valid contact-IK pose.

Instead, the current implementation solves one grasp IK problem for \(X_{WC,0}^{\mathrm{IK}}\), producing \(q_{h,\mathrm{live}}\), and a second for \(X_{WC,N}^{\mathrm{IK}}\), producing \(q_{h,\mathrm{live,end}}\). It then linearly fills the intermediate hand-joint references:

\[ q_{h,k}^{\mathrm{des}} = \left(1-\frac{k}{N}\right)q_{h,\mathrm{live}} + \frac{k}{N}q_{h,\mathrm{live,end}}, \qquad k=0,\ldots,N. \]

With the defaults \(N=5\) and \(\Delta t=0.05\,\mathrm{s}\), “end” is 0.25 s ahead. This is a deliberate efficiency approximation: it uses two nonlinear IK solves per update rather than one at each of the six state knots. It is exact at the endpoints but only approximate at intermediate knots; solving IK at every clamped \(X_{WC,k}^{\mathrm{IK}}\) would be the more geometrically faithful, more expensive alternative. Meanwhile, the osc executor smoothly ramps between successive current live-IK updates. The IK is warm-started from the prior solution; if a solve fails, the last valid target is retained rather than feeding a failed iterate to the controller.

The useful mental model

Ghost: where the task wants the cube. Opaque cube: where the simulated cube is. Clamped IK pose: the nearest safe point between them that the fingers may chase. The three coincide during good tracking; they separate when the commanded motion is ahead of what the grasp can physically achieve.

07 · Contact topology

Releasing or reintroducing a finger rebuilds the controller around the active set.

Optional middle or ring release starts from a four-finger triangle: index and ring at the −Y base, middle at the −Y apex, thumb at +Y. Releasing a finger changes the active contact topology; the program reconstructs the C3 system rather than merely zeroing a force coordinate.

  • The outgoing finger can be moved out of contact while the remaining active fingers continue to hold.
  • Ring regrasp follows an arc, waits for proximity/contact conditions, then dwells before activating the new set.
  • Topology changes receive a temporary multisolve warm start, and each active finger's planned normal force is cross-faded after a rebuild.
  • The holding posture penalty applies only to the currently active fingers.
Controller boundary

Contact-set changes are explicit controller rebuild events. They are not represented as a continuous, fixed-dimension optimization through making and breaking every contact.

08 · Gait

The gait is a staged, contact-aware finger relay.

--gait requires both --track_cube_contact and --exec_mode=osc; the program rejects other executor modes for a gait. It overrides ordinary cube-motion commands. The triangle scheme starts with four contacts and walks ring, middle, and index in sequence. The narrower relay scheme starts from index–middle–thumb support, parks ring during rotation, and currently exercises the ring-engage leg as a temporary-holder slice.

Why the gait needs osc

A gait is not only a new cube reference. At different stages it must command one named finger to lift, follow an arc, seek a new contact point, or stay parked in free space, while the other active fingers continue holding the cube. The osc executor is the branch that directly replaces that finger's four-joint segment of \(q_h^{\mathrm{des}}\) with the gait trajectory, drives it with joint PD and gravity compensation, and blends its Jacobian-transpose normal-force term back in when the finger rejoins the active contact set.

track_cube_contact supplies the other necessary half: it continually re-solves the holding fingers' contact posture against the moving cube. Without it, the fingers that are supposed to hold would keep aiming at their old world-frame contact points while the gait asks the cube to rotate.

direct only replays \(s_u\tilde{u}_0\), so it has no mechanism to steer an individual released finger through the gait path. The post-release task_space path is implemented for its fixed three fingertip loop and does not support the release/topology behavior used by the gait. The requirement is therefore an implementation constraint, not a claim that osc is a full operational-space controller.

\[ R_{\mathrm{des}}(t) = R_{\mathrm{start}}R_y\bigl(\theta(t)\bigr), \qquad \theta(t)\in[0,\mathtt{gait\_delta}], \qquad \mathtt{gait\_delta}=0.7854\ \mathrm{rad}. \]

The default gait scheme is triangle, with a 45° nominal cube-y rotation per cycle and two cycles. Regrasp paths use a three-knot joint trajectory through an outward waypoint. Contact detection, geometric proximity, dwell checks, live destination re-aiming, and timeouts determine whether each stage can advance; every completed topology change rebuilds the local controller. The current relay implementation is best run with one cycle because its isolated plan contains the ring-engage leg, not the full index/middle/disengage relay.

11 · Friction-plan execution

A planned normal force makes friction feasible; it does not command the planned friction force.

For a contact (i), C3 represents the planned contact force as a normal component plus a weighted sum of discrete tangent directions:

\[ f_i^{\mathrm{C3}} = \lambda_{n,i}\hat n_i + \sum_{a\in\mathcal{N}_i}\beta_{i,a}\hat d_{i,a}. \]

Here (hat n_i) is the inward press direction for the fingertip, (hat d_{i,a}) are the friction-pyramid directions in the tangent plane, (lambda_{n,i}) is the planned normal force, and (eta_{i,a}) are the planned tangential-force coefficients. The set (mathcal{N}_i) means “the tangent-direction indices belonging to fingertip (i)”; it is not a numerical value or a contact normal.

The friction constraint is an availability limit:

\[ \beta_{i,a}\geq0, \qquad \sum_{a\in\mathcal{N}_i}\beta_{i,a} \leq \mu\lambda_{n,i}. \]

Equivalently, in a smooth-cone picture, the physical tangential contact force satisfies

\[ \lVert f_{t,i}\rVert \leq \mu f_{n,i}, \qquad\text{not}\qquad \lVert f_{t,i}\rVert = \mu f_{n,i}. \]
Physical interpretation

Squeezing harder enlarges the amount of friction that could be produced. It does not demand that the contact produce that friction. A stationary cube uses only the tangential force needed to oppose gravity and other disturbances; it reaches the Coulomb limit only when it is about to slip.

Why normal-only OSC can still let the cube fall

In this grasp, the fingertips mostly squeeze the cube sideways. Their normal forces are therefore mostly horizontal. The upward support that keeps the cube from falling is mainly the tangential/friction part of the planned contact force, so C3's predicted vertical resultant contains the (eta) terms:

\[ F_{z}^{\mathrm{C3}} = \sum_i \left( \lambda_{n,i}\hat n_i +\sum_{a\in\mathcal{N}_i}\beta_{i,a}\hat d_{i,a} \right)_z. \]

The current osc executor does not map this complete planned wrench to joint torque. It applies gravity compensation, joint PD, and only the planned normal-direction term:

\[ \tau_{\mathrm{osc}} = \tau_g + \tau_{\mathrm{PD}} + \sum_i J_i^{\mathsf T} \left(\lambda_{n,i}^{\mathrm{C3}}\hat n_i\right). \]

The cube turns only a small amount at a time. Yaw then pauses so the hand can repair its grasp before the next rotation. Three fingers remain planted while one finger becomes the moving, or swing, finger. A new contact is not accepted until simulator-resolved force confirms it.

Not all fingers move every time

Every small yaw creates a decision point, not a requirement to move all four fingers. The controller advances whichever finger most needs recentering. Ring may remain engaged across several micro-cycles so that primary fingers can move one at a time; it does not have to withdraw after every δ.

Where the contact targets come from

There are two different target types, and they should not be confused.

Final targets

The required 90° boundary state fixes these points. Place the cube in its final orientation and determine which cube material point lies beneath each required nominal fingertip world position. The optimizer is not allowed to move these terminal contacts somewhere more convenient.

Temporary footholds

These are stepping-stone contacts used inside the 90° unit. They are selected from safe interior regions on the numbered faces and ranked according to reachability, stability, future usefulness, and ease of reaching the fixed final targets.

Optional technical form: with a consistent surface-contact convention, the final cube-body contact for finger i is

\[ p_{C,i}^{\rm final} = \left(R_{WC}^{\rm final}\right)^T \left(p_{W,i}^{*}-p_{WC}^{*}\right). \]

If pW,i* represents a rounded fingertip center instead of its surface contact, the fingertip-radius offset must be handled first. Target construction, SAP verification, and C3 must all use the same convention.

Generating temporary footholds

Each possible contact is stored as a numbered face plus two coordinates on that face, for example, “face 2/blue, slightly above and left of center.” This identity remains attached to the rotating cube. Candidate points are sampled only from the safe interior after removing an edge band large enough for the complete fingertip patch.

Hard failures are not costs

A collision, wrong numbered face, unreachable posture, inadequate edge clearance, excessive required torque, or inability to hold the cube removes a candidate completely. The cost function J ranks only candidates that already pass every must-pass safety and feasibility check.

What the cost function J should prefer

The planner should compare complete small-step schedules rather than selecting index, middle, thumb, or ring targets independently. A good index point is useless if it blocks the only feasible middle point or prevents the later thumb transfer.

Every landing point must make the next yaw easier

A contact may look comfortable while the cube is paused and become poor immediately after the next yaw. For each surviving candidate, the planner should pretend the finger lands there, simulate the next small yaw with that material contact sticking, and inspect the resulting joint posture, edge distance, collision clearance, force direction, and torque. A short-sighted contact that immediately requires another emergency move is rejected or strongly penalized.

How a finger crosses onto the next numbered face

Index and middle first creep across safe points on yellow. As yaw accumulates, blue becomes more exposed. There is normally a range of angles where the old yellow contact is still safe and at least one blue interior point is already reachable. This is the local overlap window for that finger.

The controller does not wait through many yaws and then make one enormous jump. The face change is one ordinary spider step performed when an adjacent-face target enters the safe reachable region. Index and middle may cross at different yaw angles. If no overlap exists, the current geometry is infeasible and needs a temporary foothold, a different finger order, a different yaw partition, or a revised ring/contact target.

The thumb uses lift-and-replant Strategy B

Thumb must change from face 3/green to face 1/red, but it is also the opposing side of the squeeze. It will therefore be fully lifted and replanted only after ring establishes a deliberately selected temporary load-bearing hold. The ring must stretch far enough to stabilize the cube, but not so close to full extension that it loses joint margin, force leverage, or torque authority.

The thumb-unload test is a hard permission gate

Four contacts before thumb release do not prove that the remaining three contacts can hold the cube. Before thumb retreats, its commanded normal force is gradually reduced toward zero. Index, middle, and ring must then demonstrate that they can hold cube translation, orientation, and gravity without relying on thumb.

If drift, slip, contact loss, excessive torque, or infeasibility appears, ramp thumb compression back up and settle the original grasp. Try a different ring point or held yaw. Thumb must never continue toward red after a failed unload test.

Selecting the special ring target

Ring candidates use the same numbered-face, edge-clearance, IK, and collision checks as other contacts. They also require a useful stabilizing force direction, enough wrench authority to replace thumb temporarily, and clearance for the complete thumb retreat and red-face approach. A visually convincing ring contact that pushes in an ineffective direction is discarded.

The ring score is the same unified plan cost with extra emphasis on joint extension, force direction, thumb-path clearance, and predicted cube drift after thumb unload. Crucially, an unsuccessful thumb-free support test is a hard rejection, not a large but acceptable cost.

The exact order of index, middle, and thumb face crossings is not permanently hard-coded. The planner chooses an order whose three-contact stance modes are all feasible. The special ring-supported thumb gate remains mandatory regardless of that order.

Planning and runtime structure

Most choices can be prepared offline. Build a small graph whose nodes contain cube-yaw bins, active fingers, numbered face identities, and body-fixed contact sites. Graph edges represent one small yaw, ring engagement, or one finger step. Remove edges that fail geometry, IK, collision, torque, edge-clearance, or stance-grasp checks. Search for a low-cost path from S(k) to S(k+1). During simulation, measured pose and SAP contact guards decide whether each planned transition is actually allowed.

Validation order before the complete gait

  1. Ring hold and thumb-unload: test candidate ring targets while thumb remains ready to recover; do not move thumb yet.
  2. One ordinary spider step: execute one paused index or middle relocation and confirm stable contact.
  3. Several same-face steps: demonstrate repeated yaw–pause–step cycles without crossing a cube edge.
  4. Index/middle face crossing: demonstrate one controlled yellow-to-blue step inside a verified overlap window.
  5. Thumb lift and replant: after a passed unload gate, demonstrate green-to-red thumb motion and recovery.
  6. Full closure: combine the verified pieces and require the exact recurring hand state at +90°.
  7. Repetition: run multiple 90° units and verify the same hand state recurs at every boundary.
Unified design recorded

One lateral reorientation unit is a complete 90° quarter-turn built from repeated yaw–pause–single-finger spider steps. Temporary contacts are face-aware, selected jointly, and tested one yaw ahead. Index and middle creep from yellow to blue. Thumb is lifted from green and replanted on red only after a deliberately stretched ring hold passes the thumb-unload support test. The unit is complete only when the full hand state recurs and cube yaw alone has advanced by 90°.
