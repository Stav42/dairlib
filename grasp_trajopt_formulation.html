Three-Finger Grasp as a Contact-Implicit Trajectory Optimization

A formal statement of the grasp-synthesis problem: fixed-horizon closure with per-finger position and force targets, a quadratic control cost, and full Stewart–Trinkle friction-cone contact constraints as C3 formulates them. Companion to the grasp-theory document and the C3 solver walkthrough.

§0

Setup, Conventions, and the Three Fingers

We synthesise a grasp of a free-floating cube with the Allegro hand by solving a single trajectory optimization over a horizon of \(N = 100\) timesteps. The ring finger is ignored entirely; only three fingers participate:

FingerSymbolTip bodyCube face (from cube_kinematics.h)
Index \(I\)link_3_tip \(-Y\) face
Middle \(M\)link_7_tip \(-Y\) face
Thumb \(T\)link_15_tip \(+Y\) face (opposing)

Index and middle press one face; the thumb opposes them on the other — the two-versus-one antipodal squeeze whose force balance is derived in §5 of the grasp-theory document. Throughout, an index \(i \in \{I, M, T\}\) ranges over the three fingers and \(k \in \{0, 1, \dots, N-1\}\) over the horizon.

State

\(\mathbf{x}_k = (\mathbf{q}_k,\ \dot{\mathbf{q}}_k)\)

\(\mathbf{q}_k\) stacks the Allegro joint angles (index, middle, thumb) and the cube's free-body pose (position + quaternion); \(\dot{\mathbf{q}}_k\) the corresponding generalised velocities. This is exactly the LCS state of §2 of the solver document.

§1

Decision Variables

The timestep \(\Delta t\) is a fixed parameter, as in C3 — not a decision variable. The optimizer solves over the full trajectory of the following:

\(\mathbf{q}_{0:N}\)   — configuration trajectory (hand joints + cube pose), inside \(\mathbf{x}_k\)

\(\mathbf{u}_{0:N-1}\)   — joint-torque controls applied to the three fingers

\(\lambda_{n,k}^{i} \equiv f_k^{i} \in \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}\)   — normal contact-force magnitude of finger \(i\)

\(\boldsymbol{\beta}_k^{i} \in \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}^{n_d}\)   — tangential friction weights, one per edge of the \(n_d\)-sided friction pyramid

\(\gamma_k^{i} \in \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}\)   — sliding-speed slack (one per contact)

\(\mathbf{x}_k^{i} \in \mathbb{R}^3\)   — fingertip contact points in the world frame, tied to \(\mathbf{q}_k\) by FK (§4)

The contact variable bundle

Following C3, all per-finger contact quantities are stacked into one vector \(\boldsymbol{\lambda}_k\), and the three fingers concatenate into the full \(\boldsymbol{\lambda}_k\) that drives the dynamics:

\[ \boldsymbol{\lambda}_k^{i} = \bigl(\,\lambda_{n,k}^{i},\ \boldsymbol{\beta}_k^{i},\ \gamma_k^{i}\,\bigr), \qquad \boldsymbol{\lambda}_k = \bigl(\boldsymbol{\lambda}_k^{I},\ \boldsymbol{\lambda}_k^{M},\ \boldsymbol{\lambda}_k^{T}\bigr). \]

The scalar \(f_k^{i}\) used in the objective is precisely the normal entry \(\lambda_{n,k}^{i}\). The tangential force is recovered from the weights as \(\mathbf{f}_{t,k}^{i} = D^{i}\boldsymbol{\beta}_k^{i}\), where the columns of \(D^{i} = [\,\mathbf{d}_1^{i}\ \cdots\ \mathbf{d}_{n_d}^{i}\,]\) are the friction-pyramid direction vectors spanning the contact tangent plane (§4).

§2

The Objective Function

The cost sums three intentions over the fixed horizon — reaching the target grasp points, exerting the target forces, and keeping the control effort small:

\[ J \;=\; \sum_{k=0}^{N-1} \Bigl[\, \underbrace{\textstyle\sum_{i} w_p\,\bigl\|\mathbf{x}_k^{i}-\mathbf{x}_\star^{i}\bigr\|^2}_{\text{position tracking}} \;+\; \underbrace{\textstyle\sum_{i} w_f\,\bigl(f_k^{i}-\alpha_i\bigr)^2}_{\text{force tracking}} \;+\; \underbrace{\mathbf{u}_k^\top R\,\mathbf{u}_k}_{\text{control cost}} \,\Bigr] \]

The targets \(\mathbf{x}_\star^{I},\mathbf{x}_\star^{M},\mathbf{x}_\star^{T}\in\mathbb{R}^3\) are the grasp contact points from GetGraspPositions (cube_kinematics.h), defined relative to the cube so \(\mathbf{x}_\star^{i} = X_{WC}(\mathbf{q}_k)\,\mathbf{r}^{i}\) tracks the cube pose; \(w_p, w_f > 0\) and \(R \succ 0\) trade off the three intentions.

Term 1 — position tracking

Drives each fingertip \(\mathbf{x}_k^{i} = \mathrm{FK}_i(\mathbf{q}_k)\) toward its target grasp point \(\mathbf{x}_\star^{i}\) — the fingers converge to the contact geometry rather than only modulating force once they touch.

Term 2 — force tracking

Drives each normal force \(f_k^{i}\) toward its force-closure target \(\alpha_i\) (§3), so the converged grasp exerts a balanced squeeze, not an arbitrary one.

Term 3 — control cost

The quadratic \(\mathbf{u}_k^\top R\,\mathbf{u}_k\) penalises joint-torque effort — the standard C3 \(R\)-cost. It regularises the controls, damps chattering, and keeps the QP well conditioned.

The cost specifies the grasp on both axes: where to touch (drive \(\mathbf{x}_k^{i}\) to \(\mathbf{x}_\star^{i}\)) and how hard to squeeze (drive \(f_k^{i}\) to \(\alpha_i\)), while the \(R\)-cost keeps the effort modest. The face pairing is fixed by the contact pairs and enforced through complementarity; the position term selects the precise point on each face.

§3

The Target Forces α from Force Closure

The targets \(\alpha_I, \alpha_M, \alpha_T\) are not free. They are the internal-force magnitudes that produce a net-zero wrench on the cube — a pure squeeze that holds without accelerating it. With the grasp matrix \(G\) of §0, the squeeze lives in its nullspace:

\[ G\,\mathbf{f}_{\text{internal}} = \mathbf{0}, \qquad \mathbf{f}_{\text{internal}} = \bigl(f^{I}\hat{\mathbf{n}}_I,\ f^{M}\hat{\mathbf{n}}_M,\ f^{T}\hat{\mathbf{n}}_T\bigr). \]

For index+middle (on \(-Y\)) opposing the thumb (on \(+Y\)), force balance along the squeeze axis gives the relation already encoded in GetGraspMagnitudes (cube_kinematics.h):

\[ \alpha_T = \alpha_I + \alpha_M, \qquad \alpha_M = \rho\,\alpha_I, \qquad \rho = \frac{a - c}{c - b}, \]

where \(a, b\) are the index/middle offsets on the \(-Y\) face and \(c\) the thumb offset on \(+Y\). One free scalar (the overall squeeze magnitude \(\alpha_I\)) is chosen large enough that friction resists gravity — \(\mu\,\alpha \gtrsim m g\) — and the remaining two targets follow. That gravity is carried by the tangential (friction) force, not the normal force, is exactly why the friction components of §4 must be modelled and not assumed (§5, Case 2).

§4

Contact & Friction — the Stewart–Trinkle Cone

Each finger's contact is modelled exactly as C3 does (§1 of the solver document): a normal force, a polyhedral (pyramid) approximation of the friction cone with explicit tangential weights, and a sliding slack — all coupled by three complementarity relations.

The friction cone and its polyhedral approximation

The exact Coulomb condition couples the tangential force magnitude to the normal force:

\[ \bigl\|\mathbf{f}_{t,k}^{i}\bigr\| \;\le\; \mu\, f_k^{i}, \qquad f_k^{i} \ge 0 \quad\text{(can only push)}. \]

C3 replaces the circular cone by an \(n_d\)-sided pyramid so the constraint is linear. The tangential force is a nonnegative combination of fixed direction vectors \(\mathbf{d}_j^{i}\) spanning the tangent plane, and the cone bound becomes a single linear "friction budget":

\[ \mathbf{f}_{t,k}^{i} = \sum_{j=1}^{n_d} \beta_{k,j}^{i}\,\mathbf{d}_j^{i} = D^{i}\boldsymbol{\beta}_k^{i}, \qquad \boldsymbol{\beta}_k^{i} \ge \mathbf{0}, \qquad \mathbf{e}^\top\boldsymbol{\beta}_k^{i} \;\le\; \mu\, f_k^{i}, \]

where \(\mathbf{e} = (1,\dots,1)^\top\). The slack in this budget, \(\mu f_k^{i} - \mathbf{e}^\top\boldsymbol{\beta}_k^{i} \ge 0\), is "remaining friction capacity"; it is the quantity complementary to sliding below.

The three complementarity relations per contact

Let \(\phi_k^{i} = \phi^{i}(\mathbf{q}_k)\) be the signed gap between tip \(i\) and its cube face, \(J_t^{i}\) the tangential contact Jacobian, and \(\gamma_k^{i}\) the sliding speed. The Stewart–Trinkle conditions are:

\(0 \le f_k^{i} \;\perp\; \phi_k^{i} \ge 0\)   — normal: push only when touching

\(0 \le \boldsymbol{\beta}_k^{i} \;\perp\; \bigl(\gamma_k^{i}\mathbf{e} + \Delta t\,J_t^{i}\dot{\mathbf{q}}_k\bigr) \ge 0\)   — tangential: friction opposes the sliding direction

\(0 \le \gamma_k^{i} \;\perp\; \bigl(\mu f_k^{i} - \mathbf{e}^\top\boldsymbol{\beta}_k^{i}\bigr) \ge 0\)   — budget: slide only when friction is saturated

Normal

Either the gap is zero (contact, force allowed) or the force is zero (separated).

Tangential

A friction weight \(\beta_{k,j}^{i}\) is active only in directions where the contact is actually sliding; \(\gamma_k^{i}\) lifts the relative velocity to be \(\ge 0\) in every pyramid direction.

Budget

The contact sticks (\(\gamma_k^{i}=0\)) while friction capacity remains; it begins to slide only once the budget \(\mu f_k^{i}-\mathbf{e}^\top\boldsymbol{\beta}_k^{i}\) hits zero.

Packaged into the LCS gap vector

Stacking the right-hand sides of the three relations for all three fingers gives the gap vector \(\boldsymbol{\eta}_k\), complementary to \(\boldsymbol{\lambda}_k\), exactly the object the solver works with:

\[ \boldsymbol{\eta}_k = E\,\mathbf{x}_k + F\,\boldsymbol{\lambda}_k + H\,\mathbf{u}_k + \mathbf{c}, \qquad 0 \;\le\; \boldsymbol{\lambda}_k \;\perp\; \boldsymbol{\eta}_k \;\ge\; 0. \]

Note the tangential row carries the fixed factor \(\Delta t\) (the timestep that scales velocity into displacement, \(E_t = \Delta t\) in the solver). Since \(\Delta t\) is a constant parameter, this row stays linear and the LCS is exactly C3's.

§5

Dynamics, Schedule & The Complete Problem

Dynamics — the linearised LCS

The nonlinear contact dynamics \(\mathbf{x}_{k+1} = f(\mathbf{x}_k,\mathbf{u}_k)\) are linearised at the current operating point by Drake AutoDiff into the constant matrices \(A, B, D, \mathbf{d}\) (§3 of the solver document), with the fixed timestep \(\Delta t\) already folded in — exactly C3's form:

\[ \mathbf{x}_{k+1} = A\,\mathbf{x}_k + B\,\mathbf{u}_k + D\,\boldsymbol{\lambda}_k + \mathbf{d}, \qquad \forall k. \]

\(D\) is the bridge that turns the contact forces \(\boldsymbol{\lambda}_k\) into cube acceleration. Because nothing in the problem multiplies two decision variables, the dynamics remain affine and the LCS is untouched relative to the stock C3 controller.

Contact schedule and fingertip kinematics

The first 80 of 100 steps are a free-space approach with no contact; the squeeze is confined to the last 20 steps. Fingertip points are tied to the configuration by forward kinematics:

\[ \boldsymbol{\lambda}_k^{i} = \mathbf{0}\ \ (k = 0,\dots,79), \qquad \mathbf{x}_k^{i} = \mathrm{FK}_i(\mathbf{q}_k). \]
Grasp-synthesis QPCC — fixed horizon, with friction cone

\(\displaystyle \min_{\substack{\mathbf{x}_{0:N},\ \mathbf{u}_{0:N-1} \\ \boldsymbol{\lambda}_{0:N-1},\ \boldsymbol{\eta}_{0:N-1}}} \ \sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\Bigl[\, \mathbf{u}_k^\top R\,\mathbf{u}_k + \sum_{i}\bigl( w_p\|\mathbf{x}_k^{i}-\mathbf{x}_\star^{i}\|^2 + w_f(f_k^{i}-\alpha_i)^2 \bigr) \,\Bigr]\)

\(\text{subject to, for all } k \text{ and } i\in\{I,M,T\}:\)

\(\quad \mathbf{x}_{k+1} = A\mathbf{x}_k + B\mathbf{u}_k + D\boldsymbol{\lambda}_k + \mathbf{d} \qquad\textbf{[dynamics]}\)

\(\quad \boldsymbol{\eta}_k = E\mathbf{x}_k + F\boldsymbol{\lambda}_k + H\mathbf{u}_k + \mathbf{c} \qquad\textbf{[gap definition]}\)

\(\quad 0 \le \boldsymbol{\lambda}_k \perp \boldsymbol{\eta}_k \ge 0 \qquad\textbf{[normal + tangential + budget complementarity]}\)

\(\quad \mathbf{e}^\top\boldsymbol{\beta}_k^{i} \le \mu f_k^{i},\quad \boldsymbol{\beta}_k^{i} \ge \mathbf{0},\quad f_k^{i}\ge 0 \qquad\textbf{[friction pyramid]}\)

\(\quad \mathbf{x}_k^{i} = \mathrm{FK}_i(\mathbf{q}_k) \qquad\textbf{[fingertip kinematics]}\)

\(\quad \boldsymbol{\lambda}_k^{i} = \mathbf{0},\quad k = 0,\dots,79 \qquad\textbf{[contact schedule]}\)

\(\quad \mathbf{x}_0 = \hat{\mathbf{x}},\quad \alpha_T = \alpha_I + \alpha_M,\quad \alpha_M = \rho\,\alpha_I \qquad\textbf{[initial state + force-closure targets]}\)

This is a Quadratic Program with Complementarity Constraints over the friction-pyramid-augmented \(\boldsymbol{\lambda}\) — the same problem class and the same LCS as the Push-Anything controller, differing only in the cost (position + force tracking plus the \(R\)-cost) and the explicit contact schedule.

§6

How It Is Solved — The C3 Mapping

The complementarity constraint is non-convex, so the problem is solved by ADMM exactly as in §4–§8 of the solver document. The friction-pyramid variables \((\boldsymbol{\beta},\gamma)\) are already part of \(\boldsymbol{\lambda}\) there, so they need no special handling. The split is:

z-update (QP)

A convex QP over \((\mathbf{x},\mathbf{u},\boldsymbol{\lambda},\boldsymbol{\eta})\) carrying the cost (now including the \(\mathbf{u}_k^\top R\,\mathbf{u}_k\) term), the linear dynamics, the gap equation, the friction-budget inequality, and the schedule \(\boldsymbol{\lambda}_k=\mathbf{0}\) for \(k<80\). Solved with OSQP.

δ-update (projection)

Projects onto \(0\le\boldsymbol{\lambda}\perp\boldsymbol{\eta}\ge0\) per step — the non-convex piece, now including the tangential and budget blocks. Reconciled with the QP by the dual \(\mathbf{w}\) until consensus.

With \(\Delta t\) fixed, the problem stays a pure QPCC — the inner solve is exactly the stock C3 z/δ/w iteration, unchanged. The only additions are the \(R\)-cost and the tracking terms in the z-update QP. The LCS matrices \(A,B,D,E,F,H,\mathbf{c},\mathbf{d}\) are rebuilt by AutoDiff at each re-linearisation, since \(G\), the gaps, and the contact Jacobians all move as the cube and fingers move (critique 4).

§7

Refinements & Cautions

The formulation above is faithful to the proposal. Three precise points make it well posed and solvable.

1

The pyramid is an inner approximation of the true cone

\(\mathbf{e}^\top\boldsymbol{\beta} \le \mu f_n\) with \(n_d\) edges under-approximates the circular cone \(\|\mathbf{f}_t\|\le\mu f_n\): the realisable friction is slightly direction-dependent and never exceeds the true cone. Use \(n_d = 4\) (fast, coarse) up to \(8\) (smoother, more variables) per contact. If you need the exact cone, the second-order-cone form is available but turns the inner solve into an SOCP rather than a QP — usually not worth it here.

2

The 80/20 schedule pre-empts what C3 is good at

Forcing \(\boldsymbol{\lambda}_k = \mathbf{0}\) for \(k < 80\) hands the optimizer a fixed contact time. That safe choice does solve "the fingers arrive together" — all three are released into contact at the same \(k\). But contact-implicit MPC's whole advantage is discovering contact timing through complementarity. If grasps fail because 20 steps is too few to build \(\alpha\), relax to a soft schedule (allow force after step 80, do not forbid it earlier) rather than hard-zeroing.

3

\(\mathbf{x}_k^{i}\) carries a cost now — the FK equality is mandatory

With the position-tracking term, the fingertip points are not decorative: the cost pulls \(\mathbf{x}_k^{i}\) toward \(\mathbf{x}_\star^{i}\). The equality \(\mathbf{x}_k^{i} = \mathrm{FK}_i(\mathbf{q}_k)\) must be enforced as a hard constraint — otherwise the optimizer satisfies the cheap position cost by floating the contact point to the target in mid-air while \(\mathbf{q}_k\) stays put. Since \(\mathbf{x}_k^{i}\) is a deterministic function of \(\mathbf{q}_k\), the cleanest formulation substitutes \(\mathrm{FK}_i(\mathbf{q}_k)\) directly into the cost (and into the gaps \(\phi^i\) and grasp matrix \(G\)), eliminating \(\mathbf{x}_k^{i}\) as a variable. \(\mathrm{FK}_i\) is nonlinear, so the position term is a nonlinear least-squares cost — linearise it at each re-linearisation alongside the LCS matrices, consistent with §6.

Bottom line

The structure is sound and maps cleanly onto the existing C3 stack — the LCS and the fixed timestep \(\Delta t\) are exactly C3's. It is the Push-Anything QPCC with (i) a position + force tracking cost plus the standard \(\mathbf{u}^\top R\,\mathbf{u}\) control cost, and (ii) explicit Stewart–Trinkle friction-pyramid variables and constraints. Pick a pyramid resolution (item 1), and enforce the schedule and FK equality (items 2–3), and the problem is well posed.

§8

Implementation Status, Problems & the Phase 1 + Phase 2 Pivot

This section records what was actually built, the problems encountered (both the solver issues we fixed and the one fundamental wall we hit), and the recommended change of approach.

8.1  ·  What we built

Current approach

We forked the c3 solver and added a force-reference cost \(\sum_k (\boldsymbol{\lambda}_k - \boldsymbol{\lambda}_k^\star)^\top W (\boldsymbol{\lambda}_k - \boldsymbol{\lambda}_k^\star)\) (methods SetForceTrackingWeight / UpdateForceTarget), weighting only the three normal-force entries so the squeeze magnitudes track \(\alpha_I,\alpha_M,\alpha_T\). A test program (allegro_grasp_c3.cc) builds the Allegro+cube plant, places the fingertips on the cube via IK, linearizes to an LCS, and runs one C3 solve with a heavy cube-pose anchor \(Q\) plus this force cost. The intent: command \(\alpha_M\) and read back the planned normal forces, then sweep \(\alpha_M\) to find the squeeze that lets friction resist gravity.

8.2  ·  Numerical problems encountered (and fixed)

Getting a single solve to run took a sequence of fixes, all symptoms of an ill-conditioned, stiff MPC for a torque-controlled hand:

SymptomRoot causeFix
OSQP "non-convex" in the ADMM projection step The projection Hessian's \(\lambda\)-block is the LCS matrix \(F\), whose symmetric part is not PSD for a frictional contact Regularize the projection solver (\(\sigma = 1.0\), equilibration) in the c3 fork
Main QP IterationLimit → zero fallback \(\lvert B\rvert \approx 1638\): torque acting on light finger joints makes a hugely ill-scaled input matrix Input rescaling \(\tilde{\mathbf{u}} = \mathbf{u}/s_u\) so \(\lvert B\rvert\!\to\!\lvert A\rvert\); scale \(R\) by \(s_u^2\)
SolverSpecificError A hand-rolled OSQP option set dropped polishing/\(\rho\)/\(\alpha\) Restore C3's tuned default options; loosen eps to 1e-3
Stiff contact dynamics Fingertip collision spheres penetrated the cube by ~17 mm Back the fingertips off to a ~3 mm contact gap

After these, the solve converges and produces nonzero contact forces — the plant → LCS → C3 → force-cost pipeline works end to end.

8.3  ·  The fundamental wall: C3 cannot command the squeeze

!

The grip squeeze is an internal force, and C3 is blind to it

The squeeze is an internal force: equal-and-opposite presses from opposing fingers that cancel, producing zero net wrench on the cube. Formally it lives in the nullspace of the grasp map \(G\) (see the grasp-theory critique).

\[ G\,\mathbf{f}_{\text{squeeze}} = \mathbf{0} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \text{the cube's motion is identical for any squeeze magnitude.} \]

C3 chooses contact forces only by how they drive the state (the cube pose), which responds solely to the net wrench. Since the squeeze produces no net wrench, C3 is structurally blind to it — it has no reason to prefer any squeeze level. Our force-reference cost tries to pin it anyway, but it is a soft preference competing against the state anchor.

Confirmed empirically:

  • Raising the force-cost weight 100× barely moved the normal forces (they crept from \(-0.16\) toward \(-0.05\), i.e. toward the target by a sliver) — the cost is wired correctly but overwhelmingly dominated.
  • Removing the state anchor to let the force cost win instead broke solvability (the anchor was also providing the regularization that makes the QP solvable) → IterationLimit → zero.

So we are in a vise: the state anchor is required for the problem to solve, yet it dictates the forces and steamrolls the squeeze command. A state-tracking MPC cannot command an internal grip force — not a bug, but the nature of the tool.

8.4  ·  The pivot: a two-phase (hybrid position/force) controller

A grasp has two sub-tasks that need opposite kinds of control, and no single controller does both well:

Sub-taskNeedsGood at itBad at it
Bring all fingers to contact simultaneously (timing) planning ahead / coordination C3 / trajectory optimization force control (fingers arrive raggedly; the first to land shoves the cube)
Squeeze with force \(\alpha\) once in contact reacting to contact with a set force force / PD control (\(\boldsymbol{\tau}=J^\top\mathbf{f}\)) C3 (squeeze is invisible, §8.3)

The resolution is the classic hybrid position/force control split — divide the grasp in time:

Phase 1 — Reach (free space)

Use a coordinated trajectory (C3 / planning) to bring all three fingertips to the cube surface arriving at the same instant. Position/ trajectory controlled; no squeezing yet. This solves the timing problem.

Phase 2 — Squeeze (in contact)

The moment all three fingers report contact, switch to force control \(\boldsymbol{\tau} = J^\top \mathbf{f}\) and command the squeeze \(\alpha\). This directly sets the grip force — the quantity C3 cannot.

The switch trigger is "all three contacts closed" (gap \(\approx 0\) / small measured force). Phase 1 covers the timing that force control is bad at; Phase 2 covers the squeeze that C3 is blind to. Each tool does only what it is good at, and the friction-vs- gravity question is then answered directly by sweeping \(\alpha\) in Phase 2 (or by simulating and observing whether the cube is held).

Status & recommendation

The C3 force-reference machinery is built and the solve works, but §8.3 shows it cannot serve as the squeeze command. Recommended next step: keep C3 (or a simpler coordinated plan) for Phase 1, and build the \(\boldsymbol{\tau}=J^\top\mathbf{f}\) Phase 2 squeeze controller with a contact-triggered handoff between them.

§9

Hybrid Controller — Exact Implementation Steps

A concrete build plan for the Phase 1 + Phase 2 controller of §8.4. It is built on top of allegro_cube_visualizer.cc, which already has the Allegro+cube plant, the Meshcat visualization, the simulation loop, and a PD joint controller. We add one controller system with two modes and a contact-triggered switch.

9.0  ·  Architecture

One system, two modes

A new HybridGraspController (a Drake LeafSystem): input port = Allegro state \((\mathbf{q},\dot{\mathbf{q}})\); output port = joint torques. It holds a discrete mode (kReach / kSqueeze) and per-finger contact flags. Each control step it computes the contact gaps, updates the mode, and emits torques from whichever phase is active. The cube pose is read from the plant context (sim) so gaps and contact normals are known exactly.

9.1  ·  Phase 1 — coordinated reach (position control)

Goal: bring all three fingertips to the cube surface without shoving the cube.

  1. Contact configuration. Compute \(\mathbf{q}_{\text{contact}}\) with SolveGraspIK (cube_kinematics.h), targeting the fingertips exactly at each face — GetGraspPositions(X_WC, cube_size, a, b, c), no inward penetration. (Penetration is Phase 2's job; targeting the surface means an early-arriving finger rests with ~0 force and does not push the cube.)
  2. Pre-grasp configuration. Compute \(\mathbf{q}_{\text{pregrasp}}\) with the fingertips offset a few cm outside the faces (GetIntermediatePosition).
  3. Synchronized trajectory. Build one cubic spline \(\mathbf{q}_{\text{ref}}(t)\): open \(\to \mathbf{q}_{\text{pregrasp}} \to \mathbf{q}_{\text{contact}}\), with a single common end time, using PiecewisePolynomial::CubicShapePreserving (as the visualizer already does). All fingers are scheduled to reach the surface together.
  4. Track it with the existing PdJointController law \(\boldsymbol{\tau} = K_p(\mathbf{q}_{\text{ref}}-\mathbf{q}) + K_d(\dot{\mathbf{q}}_{\text{ref}}-\dot{\mathbf{q}})\), plus gravity compensation (9.3, step 4) so the fingers do not sag.
Why this fixes the timing

Targets are at the surface, so contact never produces a shove — fingers that arrive early simply rest there. The Phase-2 switch (9.2) waits until all three are touching, so residual timing differences are absorbed by waiting rather than requiring perfect simultaneity.

9.2  ·  The handoff trigger (contact detection)

  1. Per-finger gap by FK. Each step, get the fingertip world position \(\mathbf{p}_i = \) plant.EvalBodyPoseInWorld(ctx, tip_i).translation() and the cube pose \(X_{WC}\). The signed gap to finger \(i\)'s face is \(\phi_i = (\mathbf{p}_i - \mathbf{p}^{\text{face}}_i)\cdot \hat{\mathbf{n}}_i\), where \(\hat{\mathbf{n}}_i\) is the inward face normal and \(\mathbf{p}^{\text{face}}_i\) the face point.
  2. Contact flag. Finger \(i\) is "in contact" when \(\phi_i \le \varepsilon\) (e.g. \(\varepsilon = 1\) mm). (Alternatively read normal force from plant.get_contact_results_output_port().)
  3. Switch. When all three flags are true for a few consecutive steps (debounce), set mode \(\to\) kSqueeze and latch it.

9.3  ·  Phase 2 — squeeze (force control, \(\boldsymbol{\tau}=J^\top\mathbf{f}\))

  1. Squeeze magnitudes. From GetGraspMagnitudes: with offsets \(\rho=1\), \(\alpha_I=\alpha_M=\) --alpha_m, \(\alpha_T=\alpha_I+\alpha_M\).
  2. Desired contact forces (world frame), pointing inward along each face normal:
    \[ \mathbf{f}_I = \alpha_I\,\hat{\mathbf{n}}_I,\quad \mathbf{f}_M = \alpha_M\,\hat{\mathbf{n}}_M,\quad \mathbf{f}_T = \alpha_T\,\hat{\mathbf{n}}_T \]
    For the axis-aligned cube: \(\hat{\mathbf{n}}_I=\hat{\mathbf{n}}_M=+\hat{\mathbf{y}}\) (−Y face), \(\hat{\mathbf{n}}_T=-\hat{\mathbf{y}}\) (+Y face). If the cube rotates, transform the cube-frame normals by \(X_{WC}\).
  3. Map forces to torques. For each finger get the fingertip translational Jacobian and accumulate:
    \[ \boldsymbol{\tau}_{\text{squeeze}} = \sum_{i\in\{I,M,T\}} J_i(\mathbf{q})^\top \mathbf{f}_i \]
    via plant.CalcJacobianTranslationalVelocity(ctx, kV, tip_i, [0,0,0], world, world, &Ji) (a \(3\times n_v\) matrix; \(J_i^\top \mathbf{f}_i\) gives an \(n_v\) torque vector).
  4. Gravity compensation. Add \(\boldsymbol{\tau}_g = -\,\)plant.CalcGravityGeneralizedForces(ctx) so the hand holds its pose against gravity.
  5. Total + ramp. \(\boldsymbol{\tau} = \boldsymbol{\tau}_{\text{squeeze}} + \boldsymbol{\tau}_g\), selecting the Allegro rows for the actuation port. Ramp \(\alpha\) from 0 to target over ~0.5 s on entering kSqueeze for a smooth grip.
Note — friction is what holds gravity

The commanded \(\mathbf{f}_i\) are horizontal squeeze (normal) forces; they do not directly resist gravity. The cube is held by friction at the contacts, bounded by \(\mu\,\alpha\). The whole point of sweeping \(\alpha\) is to find where \(\mu\cdot(\text{total normal force}) \gtrsim m g\) (analytically \(\alpha_M \approx 0.6\) N for \(m=0.2\) kg, \(\mu=0.8\)).

  2. Replace the PD-only controller with HybridGraspController. Connect plant.get_state_output_port(allegro_index) → controller.state and controller.torques → plant.get_actuation_input_port(allegro_index).
  3. Give the controller the plant + a context handle (or pass the cube pose in) so it can evaluate FK, gaps, Jacobians, and gravity each step.
  4. Expose --alpha_m, \(K_p,K_d\), the contact threshold \(\varepsilon\), and the squeeze ramp time as gflags.

9.5  ·  Test & the α sweep

  1. Run with Meshcat open. Watch Phase 1: all three fingers reach the cube together and rest.
  2. Watch the switch to Phase 2 and the grip ramp in.
  3. Sweep --alpha_m (e.g. 0.3 → 2.0 N). For each, observe whether the cube is held or slips out under gravity. The smallest \(\alpha_m\) that holds is the empirical friction threshold — compare to the analytic \(\approx 0.6\) N.
  4. Optional: enable the contact-force visualizer to confirm the normal forces match \(\alpha\).
Deliverable

One new file (e.g. allegro_grasp_hybrid.cc) + a HybridGraspController system + a BUILD target. Phase 1 reuses the existing IK / spline / PD pieces; Phase 2 is the \(J^\top\mathbf{f}\) + gravity-compensation law. The contact-triggered switch ties them together. This finally makes \(\alpha_m\) a direct, sweepable grip command — the thing C3 could not provide.
