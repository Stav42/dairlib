Contact Location & LCS Linearization

Two questions, answered in code order: where does Drake decide a finger touches the cube, and how does that become the LCS's linear matrices?

1. Picking the contact pair

GetNClosestContactPairs runs a signed-distance query on every candidate pair and keeps the N closest. A plain closest-N pick, done once at the current state.

2. Picking the contact point

Fingertip collision shape = 12mm sphere. Cube collision shape = a single box (not the visual meshes).

URDF sphere: allegro_hand_description_right.urdf  ·  cube box: numbered_cube.sdf:78-90

Sphere vs. box → general path, not the sphere-vs-mesh special case:

query_object.ComputeSignedDistancePairClosestPoints(geometry_id_A_, geometry_id_B_);

geom_geom_collider.cc:113-121 (called from GetGeometryQueryResult, line 126)

This is Drake's real proximity engine — true closest points between the sphere and box, radius already subtracted. Returns:

FieldMeaning
distancethe true signed gap (this becomes φ)
nhat_BA_Wcontact normal, world frame
p_ACa / p_BCbwitness points — the pair of closest points, one on the sphere's surface (p_ACa) and one on the box's surface (p_BCb), that together realize the signed distance above. When the shapes are separated they're the two nearest surface points; in penetration they're the two points of deepest overlap.
This is the actual computation φ/η come from — not a fixed local-frame offset point. That's why the earlier hand-rolled FK-distance table didn't match η: different computation entirely.

3. Contact Jacobian

PolytopeContactEvaluator::Eval → GeomGeomCollider::EvalPolytope:

  1. Re-runs the same query for nhat_BA_W.
  2. Builds a contact frame: RotationMatrix::MakeFromOneVector(nhat_BA_W, 0).
  3. Builds the friction-cone basis — row 0 = normal, rows 1..2n = tangent rays at θ = i·π/n.
  4. Jacobians at the witness points (not body origin) — so the Jacobian reflects velocity right where contact actually occurs: CalcJacobianTranslationalVelocity(..., p_ACa, ...) and (..., p_BCb, ...), differenced, projected through the force basis.

geom_geom_collider.cc:156-220

Row 0 of the result → Jn. Remaining rows → Jt.

lcs_factory.cc:263-288 (ComputeContactJacobian)

4. Linearizing the dynamics

Two separate pieces, both inside GenerateLCS:

(a) Smooth dynamics — autodiff

f(q,v,u) = M(q)⁻¹(τ_g + τ_u + f_app − C(q,v))

Evaluated on an AutoDiffXd plant at the current (q*,v*,u*); gradients extracted directly:

Jf_q, Jf_v, Jf_u = ∂f/∂q, ∂f/∂v, ∂f/∂u

A real first-order Taylor expansion, not a hand-derived formula. Builds the contact-free A, B, d.

The one fact that matters

φ, Jn, Jt are computed once, at x₀, from a real geometry query. Every LCS matrix built from them is then frozen and reused for every knot in the horizon. The ADMM/QP solve never re-queries geometry until the next relinearization.

This is the exact mechanism behind everything seen in the diagnostics: FK gap growing with k, η disagreeing with a geometric distance at k0, the general "single linearization over the horizon" staleness — all the same fact, now pinned to exact lines.
